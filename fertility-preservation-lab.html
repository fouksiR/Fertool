Why start with endometriosis? ~2.5 min

Because it’s the everyday case where treatment can cost eggs

A common diagnosis, a common operation, a measurable effect on reserve — and a Medicare-rebatable indication.
Eggs are a finite reserve — they can be banked, but never replaced. When a condition, or the treatment that fixes it, is likely to reduce that reserve, freezing eggs first keeps a future pregnancy possible. That is medical egg freezing, in one sentence.

Medicare: a medical indication changes the cost

Medically indicated egg freezing (endometriosis and the other conditions in this talk) attracts Medicare rebates — including PBS-subsidised stimulation medications, not just part of the cycle cost. Elective freezing is fully self-funded. Worth telling patients.

A nuance for tomorrow's consults
Low AMH ≠ low natural fertility
AMH (anti-Müllerian hormone) counts how many eggs are left — not her chance of conceiving naturally. What it does predict: eggs per freezing or IVF cycle — which is what surgery may affect.

Hormonal suppression after surgery adds waiting time

After excision, ovarian suppression (a GnRH agonist or continuous hormonal therapy) is sometimes used to settle disease and reduce recurrence. It isn’t routine — but when it is used, conception stays on hold for those extra months, on top of surgical recovery. One more reason the timeline conversation belongs before surgery.

A timing guide, not a rule
Chemo / radiotherapy▼
Time-critical
Cancer treatment — and strong immune drugs like cyclophosphamide. Oncofertility teams can start within days — ideally before the first dose.
Breast cancer · lymphomaPelvic radiotherapyCyclophosphamide
Genetic risk of ovarian insufficiency▼
Refer young
Fragile X carriers (FMR1 — about 1 in 5 develop premature ovarian insufficiency) and Turner mosaic (45,X/46,XX). The window is the teens to mid-20s. A family history of ovarian insufficiency before 40 counts too.

Low egg reserve for her age▼
Weeks
Unexpectedly low AMH in a woman delaying pregnancy. Her natural fertility may be fine (step 2) — but if she will ever freeze, earlier banks more.
Surgery on the ovary▼
Before surgery
Repeat cyst surgery, planned ovary removal, BRCA risk-reducing surgery. A fertility opinion beforehand keeps options open.
Gender-affirming care▼
Before starting
Discuss fertility before long-term testosterone or surgery — a standard part of consent.
How to carry it into clinic ~1 min

Three questions to ask yourself

The quick screen

Think O·R·T before treatment

O
Ovary — in the treatment field?

Ovarian surgery, chemo, pelvic radiotherapy.

R
Reserve — low, or at genetic risk?

Low AMH for age, both ovaries involved, prior surgery, fragile X, Turner mosaic. Unknown? Measure it.

T
Time — is pregnancy years away?

Age ≥35, or plans deferred. Longer runway = today's reserve matters more.

Any one YES → raise egg freezing in the consult.  O plus another YES → consider fertility input first.
