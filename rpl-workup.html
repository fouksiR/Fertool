Why think in yields? ~2 min

Because testing smart beats testing everything

Even after a complete workup, about half of couples get no answer. Knowing each test’s expected yield prevents both the missed treatable cause and the expensive futile panel.
Every test in the RPL workup has a published chance of finding something — its yield. A handful are worth ordering for everyone; many popular ones find nothing actionable at all.

The four-test GP panel fits one request form

Lupus anticoagulant + anticardiolipin (± anti-β2GPI), TSH + TPO antibodies, and a 3D transvaginal ultrasound. Confirm any positive APS result ≥12 weeks later before labelling.

The pearl that reframes the consult
"No cause found" is not "nothing to offer"
Unexplained RPL carries a good prognosis — most couples go on to a successful pregnancy with supportive care alone. The workup’s job is to catch the treatable minority and to earn that reassurance for everyone else.

Antiphospholipid screen▼
confirmed 1–5%
Lupus anticoagulant + anticardiolipin IgG/IgM (reasonable to add anti-β2GPI on the same form). The one treatable serological cause — but most first positives do not persist: ~10% test positive once, only 1–5% remain positive on the mandatory ≥12-week repeat. Never label APS off a single draw. If confirmed APS: low-dose aspirin + prophylactic LMWH from positive βhCG. Not valid on warfarin/DOACs; withhold heparin 12 h pre-sample.
ESHRE Rec 15–16RANZCOG R8
TSH + TPO antibodies▼
treatable ~5%
If TSH abnormal → add free T4. Treat from TSH >4 (~5% of RPL patients). TPO antibodies are positive in ~10% — similar to background — and euthyroid TPO-positivity does not benefit from levothyroxine (ESHRE 2022 update); recheck TSH at 7–9 weeks in the next pregnancy.
ESHRE Rec 24, 47, 51RANZCOG R15–16
3D transvaginal ultrasound▼
yield ~13%
Request "TVS with 3D reconstruction" — congenital anomalies in ~13% of recurrent miscarriage vs 5.5% of all women (Chan 2011); also screens for adenomyosis. Sonohysterography is an acceptable alternative; MRI only if 3D unavailable. Anomaly found → assess renal tract. Note: septum resection is no longer routine after the Rikken RCT.
ESHRE Rec 33–38RANZCOG R8
POC array — plan it for the next loss▼
yield ~55%
Array-CGH on miscarriage tissue explains the individual loss (aneuploidy in ~55%) and spares futile maternal testing. Explanatory, not predictive — counsel before testing. Arrange the pathway now so the sample isn’t lost in the moment.
ESHRE Rec 11–12Lei 2022
Parental karyotype — tailor, don’t reflex▼
yield 2–4%
Balanced translocation in ~2–4% of RPL couples — but under 2% when she is over 39 with fewer than three losses and no family history (Franssen 2005). Best reserved for younger couples, abnormal POC, family history, or consanguinity. Abnormal → genetic counselling; natural conception outcomes remain good.
ESHRE Rec 13Franssen 2005
Sperm DNA fragmentation — selected men▼
diagnostic only
Consider in unexplained RPL, particularly with smoking, alcohol, obesity or older paternal age. Drives lifestyle counselling — no treatment has RCT support. Private-only in Australia (~$200–300).
ESHRE Rec 39–40
The refuse-politely list▼
yield ≈ 0 actionable
Inherited thrombophilia (FVL, prothrombin, protein C/S) — anticoagulation does not improve live birth; test only for independent VTE indications. MTHFR — common, benign, standard folate suffices. NK cells / HLA / cytokines — no therapeutic implication. AMH for RPL — different question entirely. Glucose/insulin, androgens, homocysteine, routine vitamin D testing, luteal-phase tests — no prognostic value; supplement vitamin D empirically and skip the blood test.

Worth prescribing in words
Supportive care is an intervention

Easy access to early scans, a named contact, and an agreed plan — couples with unexplained RPL managed this way do well, with most achieving a live birth without any drug at all. Add empirical vitamin D 1000–2000 IU daily (skip the blood test), and lifestyle work for both partners.

What not to promise
No empirical aspirin, heparin or progesterone-for-all
Aspirin + LMWH helps confirmed APS — not unexplained RPL. Progesterone’s benefit is limited to women with bleeding in early pregnancy and prior losses (PRISM/PROMISE). Blanket prescriptions medicalise without helping.
